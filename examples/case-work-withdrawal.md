# Withdrawal after a workplace change

Minh is a fictional 34-year-old project coordinator. During the last two months, coworkers have noticed that Minh speaks less in meetings and often eats lunch alone. The change followed a reorganization in which Minh received a new manager and several familiar teammates moved to other groups.

Minh told one coworker, “I prepare carefully, but in meetings I keep thinking that anything I say will sound foolish.” Minh has declined two optional social events and recently missed one internal deadline. At the same time, Minh continues to complete routine tasks and received positive feedback from a client.

The case does not provide information about Minh’s earlier relationships, childhood, medical history, sleep, appetite, life outside work, or how Minh personally understands the change. It is unclear whether the withdrawal is limited to work, whether the new manager has criticized Minh, or whether other pressures occurred during the same period.

Questions for the learner:

1. Which observations can be treated as facts, and which explanations remain hypotheses?
2. What evidence would help distinguish cognitive, behavioral, relational, and existential interpretations?
